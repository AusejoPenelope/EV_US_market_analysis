# 🔋 U.S. Electric Vehicle Market Share Analysis

## 📋 Project Overview
This project analyzes the distribution and adoption of electric vehicles (EVs) 
across the United States. Using vehicle registration data by state and fuel type, 
the goal is to uncover where EV adoption is happening fastest, how EVs compare 
to traditional fuels, and what insights can be drawn for policymakers and automakers.

## 🎯 Business Questions
- What percentage of vehicles in each state are EVs, PHEVs, HEVs, and gasoline?
- Which states have the highest EV adoption rates, and which lag behind?
- Which alternative fuels are significant vs. niche?
- Where should policymakers prioritize EV infrastructure investment?

## 🛠️ Tools used

### 🔍 Key Findings
- **California** leads EV adoption at 3.41%, nearly 3 times the national average (1.30%)
- **North Dakota** and **Mississippi** have the lowest adoption at just 0.13%
- Gasoline still dominates in every state, representing 80-90% of all vehicles
- HEVs are the most adopted alternative fuel after EVs in most states
- Biodiesel, ethanol, and hydrogen remain niche fuels nationally

## Recommendations
Florida, Texas, and New York represent the highest-priority states for EV infrastructure investment based on two key factors: high absolute EV registrations combined with below-average adoption rates compared to the national average (1.30%).

🔵 Florida: 254,900 EVs | 1.37% adoption 
Despite being the 3rd largest EV market by volume, Florida's adoption rate barely exceeds the national average. Its large population and sunny climate make it ideal for accelerated EV growth with the right infrastructure support.

🔵 Texas: 230,100 EVs | 0.89% adoption
The largest infrastructure gap in the country. Texas has the 2nd highest EV count nationally, but its adoption rate is 32% below the national average, a clear sign that charging infrastructure is not keeping pace with demand.

🔵 New York: 131,300 EVs | 1.16% adoption
Dense urban corridors and high commuter traffic make New York a high-impact investment target. Closing the gap to California levels could add hundreds of thousands of EV users.
